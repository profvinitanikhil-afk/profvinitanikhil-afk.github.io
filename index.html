Commerce III – Management: Principles & Functions

Master modern managerial strategies combined with ancient Indian Ethos, Fayol's principles, Drucker's dimensions, and essential organizational functions.

Course Description & Outcomes

Comprehensive overview of management in modern organizations.

Course Description

This course provides a deep understanding of concepts, principles, and functions of management. It weaves together classical principles with management lessons from the Bhagavad Gita and Indian ethos.

Course Objectives

  • Provide an overview of management within an organization.
  • Understand core principles and functions (Planning, Organizing, Directing, Controlling).
  • Develop insights into applying management principles in daily life.

Course Outcomes (COs)

CO1 (Understand): Learners will understand the fundamental concepts and principles of management.

CO2 (Apply): Learners will learn to apply management functions in daily decisions and corporate life.

University Prescribed Syllabus

Split into 2 primary modules and 4 core units (30 total lectures)

Module 1: Introduction to Management, Planning & Controlling

15 Lectures

Unit I – Introduction to Management

8 Lectures
  • Management concept, nature, and core functions of management.
  • Essential Managerial skills (Technical, Human, Conceptual).
  • Henry Fayol’s 14 principles of management.
  • Peter Drucker’s dimensions of management.
  • Chanakya’s principles of management.
  • Ten key management lessons from the Bhagavad Gita.
  • Indian Ethos and its significance to modern business management.

Unit II – Planning and Controlling

7 Lectures
  • Planning: meaning and steps in the planning process.
  • Management by Objectives (MBO): concept and advantages.
  • Management by Exception (MBE): concept and advantages.
  • Management Information System (MIS): concept & components.
  • Controlling: meaning, steps in control process, essentials of good control, and controlling techniques.

Module 2: Organising and Directing

15 Lectures

Unit III – Organising

8 Lectures
  • Features of line, line-and-staff, matrix, and virtual organisation structures.
  • Bases of departmentation (Function, Product, Territory, Customer).
  • Span of management: concept and factors influencing it.
  • Delegation of authority: concept and principles.
  • Centralisation versus decentralisation of authority.

Unit IV – Directing

7 Lectures
  • Managerial communication: core functions and major barriers.
  • Modern digital tools used by managers for communication.
  • Motivation: concept, factors, and importance in workplace.
  • Leadership: concept, styles (Autocratic, Democratic, Laissez-Faire), and key leadership qualities.

Unit-wise Self-Assessment Quiz

Test your knowledge with model answers provided in the syllabus.

Unit I

Q: What is management?

Answer: Management is the process of planning, organising, directing and controlling resources to achieve organizational objectives efficiently and effectively.

Unit I

Q: Mention any two managerial skills.

Answer: Technical skill and Human/Interpersonal skill (Conceptual skill is also valid).

Unit II

Q: What is Management by Objectives (MBO)?

Answer: MBO is a strategic management model where managers and employees jointly set goals, define responsibilities, and evaluate performance against these shared objectives.

Unit II

Q: What is Management by Exception (MBE)?

Answer: MBE is a management technique focused on identifying and handling only significant deviations or exceptions from standard performance.

Unit III

Q: Define Delegation of Authority.

Answer: The process of assigning responsibility and granting authority by a superior to a subordinate while retaining ultimate accountability.

Unit IV

Q: Name two prominent leadership styles.

Answer: Autocratic Leadership and Democratic Leadership (Laissez-faire is also a common style).

(a) Explain Line, Line-and-Staff, Matrix, and Virtual organization structures. (7.5 Marks)

(b) Explain barriers to managerial communication and discuss the role of motivation and leadership in directing employees. (7.5 Marks)
